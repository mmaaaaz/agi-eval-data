01Findings

Every figure below is computed from the dataset embedded in this page. Accuracy means correct under the frozen rule in section 06, counted per question over the full evaluated set; nothing is excluded from the denominator.

Headline

InternVL3.5-8B 36.9% vs DeepSeek-VL2-Small 29.6%

A gap of 7.3 points, and it survives every check applied: the pipeline's own score (37.3% / 30.9%), this rule, and partial credit (38.7% / 31.0%).

Neither model reaches 40%: both fail roughly two of every three questions.

Grader quality

The pipeline's flag agrees on 98.0% / 97.4% of questions

Where it differs, it errs both ways: it credits 2980 / 5039 answers this rule rejects, and rejects 2110 / 1643 it accepts. The effects partly cancel in the headline (37.3 → 36.9, 30.9 → 29.6).

Per domain they do not: projectile_motion moves 70.4 → 85.2, clock_reading 35.2 → 25.9 (InternVL).

Publish blocker

11 domain-levels score 0.0% for both models

4 are grading failures: ground truth stored as {'time_of_flight_s': 3.9, 'range_m': 34.5} or ['blue', 'purple'], which a string grader cannot read. This rule recovers them — depth_height L3, fbd L3, fbd L4, projectile_motion L4.

6 are semicolon answers like no; new value 25 where models supply one part (up to 42.0% of parts present). 1 is genuinely wrong on both models.

Level design

6 levels have one answer for all 1,500 images

cube_net L1 is always 6, gear_train L2 opposite, optical_illusion L1 2, orthographic L5 front and side, polyhedron L5 no, symmetry_pattern L5 yes. Blind answering scores 100% on all six.

Models profit: 85.2% / 46.3% over those 9000 questions. Dropping the three constant L5 levels cuts the apparent L5 rebound from 7.1 to 3.0 points (InternVL) and from 3.3 to 1.4 (DeepSeek).

Difficulty curve

Accuracy falls to L4 on both models

InternVL: 52.2 → 48.8 → 33.0 → 21.8 → 28.9. DeepSeek: 37.1 → 44.3 → 27.3 → 18.0 → 21.3.

L4 is the real difficulty peak; L5 only looks easier because of the constant levels above. DeepSeek is also weaker on L1 than L2 (37.1% vs 44.3%), consistent with its 15.5% unparsed-response rate against 0.6% for InternVL.

Baseline

Always answering the most common answer scores 13.8%

InternVL's 36.9% is +23.2 above that floor, DeepSeek's +15.8. Family floors run from 8.3% to 17.6%, so small answer spaces flatter raw accuracy.

Families

Mechanical and solid-geometry tasks lead; search and estimation trail

InternVL: Physical & Mechanical 46.4%, Solid Geometry 45.2%, weakest Analytic 26.1%. DeepSeek: best Solid Geometry 36.6%, weakest Analytic 20.3%.

Best domains: projectile_motion 85.2, cube_net 64.9, polyhedron 62.3. Worst: hex_pathfinding 9.7, embedded_figures 11.7, coordinate_geometry 13.6, impossible_object 17.6 — open-ended search and metric estimation on a reference frame.

Head to head

InternVL leads where mechanics dominate; DeepSeek wins 9 of 34 domains

Largest InternVL margins: projectile_motion +41.1, gear_train +37.8, depth_height +24.4, fbd +21.4. DeepSeek's wins concentrate in impossible_object +9.2, hex_pathfinding +7.4, orthographic +5.5, fold_punch +5.4.

Both sit under 20% on the same hard core: hex_pathfinding, embedded_figures, coordinate_geometry, impossible_object.

06Method, verification and caveats

The frozen grading rule
  1. Answer text is lower-cased; punctuation, quoting, bracket and whitespace variation is normalised away.
  2. Text answers must appear as a whole word or phrase — a match may not sit inside a longer word. This stops ground truth connected from matching inside disconnected.
  3. Numeric answers must appear in the model's committed answer (after its final-answer marker, if it emits one) within 1% relative or 0.05 absolute tolerance.
  4. Multi-part ground truth (semicolon-separated) requires every part; the share matched is reported as partial credit.
  5. Structured ground truth (dict or list literals) is parsed: numbers must match numbers, string entries match by token, and ranked lists must be in the given order.
  6. Anything still failing is wrong. Nothing is dropped from the denominator.
Verification performed
  • Raw counts recounted with grep/awk, independent of the Python pipeline: 252,505 records per model and 94,121 / 78,098 "correct": true — identical.
  • Zero duplicate question ids; all 252,505 records carry "error": null; ground truth byte-identical across runs, so the comparison is paired.
  • Levels exactly balanced at 50,501 questions each; per-domain spot checks reproduce the tables.
  • All 11772 grading disagreements are categorised and sampled (section 05) rather than assumed away.
  • Every figure in this page's text is computed from the embedded dataset at build time — nothing is transcribed by hand.
Exact vs rule-dependent
  • Exact: all counts, per-domain / level / family accuracies, oracle baselines, single-answer detection and coverage — integer counts over complete files.
  • Rule-dependent: accuracy itself, because "correct" needs a stated comparison rule. The rule above is frozen and published; the pipeline's looser rule gives 37.3% / 30.9%, and that spread is the honest uncertainty on any single number here.
  • Estimate: the sampling error below is statistical, not measured, and assumes the image subset was drawn at random.
Caveats for the paper
  • Half sample. Files are named *_dataset_3000 but hold 1,500 distinct images each (ids span 1–3,000). Confirm intent; full-set numbers may shift.
  • angle_estimation carries 1,501 images in both runs — one more than every other domain.
  • Accuracy is per question (5 per image), so repeated question formats dominate some domains.
  • If the subset is random, sampling error at n=7,500 is roughly ±1.1 points (95%); domain gaps under ~1.5 points are noise. projectile_motion (n=1,000) is ±3 points.
  • The shipped pipeline still mis-scores the 4 structured zero-levels and gives no credit on 6 multi-part levels; both need fixing before per-domain numbers are published.
